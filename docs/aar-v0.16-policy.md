# v0.16 AAR policy

VTSD v0.16 provides a generic simulator AAR procedure. The aircraft registry retains tanker and receiver system methods, including Boom, CenterlineDrogue, WingDrogue, BoomDrogueAdapter, BoomReceptacle, and Probe. Those fields remain useful metadata and support conservative flow-rate selection and future method-aware behavior. They do not gate operational eligibility in v0.16.

An AAR pair is eligible when both enabled registry profiles exist, the tanker profile has `CanTanker`, and the receiver profile has `CanReceive`. The server does not yet model physical compatibility between a tanker system and a receiver system. For example, a Probe receiver can enter the v0.16 procedure with a Boom tanker. This is not a claim that every aircraft physically supports every other aircraft.

For each side, v0.16 uses the minimum confirmed positive aircraft-specific flow limit in that profile when one or more such values exist. If none exists, it uses the generic simulated AAR fallback of 10 kg/s. The effective flow is the lower of the tanker and receiver limits. The fallback is not a confirmed aircraft, boom, or drogue value. Registry provenance is retained.

The default capture envelope is 12–160 m behind, up to 18 m lateral, 2–32 m below, and at most 4 m/s relative speed, stable for 1 second. Release hysteresis is 6–210 m behind, up to 28 m lateral, up to 48 m below, and at most 7 m/s, with a 250 ms release debounce. These broad limits are a procedural and safety envelope designed to tolerate VATSIM/TacticalLink latency, simulator update rates, scheduling and interpolation delay, position jitter, aircraft reference-point differences, frame timing, and temporary dropped samples. They use aircraft reference positions and are not intended to model boom-tip/receptacle contact, basket/probe engagement, hose extension, physical nozzle position, or aircraft-specific refueling contact points.

`Contact` means the receiver has remained sufficiently stable inside the coarse server-approved region. It does not mean physical refueling hardware has connected. The server preserves the tanker-controlled procedure: accept, `CLEAR_ASTERN`, astern positioning, `CLEAR_CONTACT`, stable geometry capture, then `Contact`. Contact never authorizes fuel by itself. The tanker must explicitly execute `START_TRANSFER`; the tanker decides when the receiver is in a suitable refueling position. Invalid geometry, stale or misaligned poses, excessive relative motion, and failed debounce checks continue to prevent or revoke contact under the existing state machine.

Future versions can add selected tanker/receiver systems, a compatibility matrix, and method-specific or aircraft-specific contact geometry without removing the registry method metadata.
